Ten ways to wire it, drawn out

System Blueprints

The same idea, owning your AI, can be built ten different ways. Here is what each one actually looks like, from a single laptop to a cluster in your closet, with the plumbing drawn and explained in plain English.

Read the diagrams like this

Each box is a piece of the system. Arrows are the path a request takes, top to bottom. Color tells you where a box lives: grey is you, green is local, coral is the engine and model on your hardware, dark is the cloud, amber is a safety gate, blue is storage. Start small at the top of the page and add pieces only when something hurts.

01

The Single Laptop

the on-ramp · one machine · zero cloud

↓localhost

↓

In plain English

It is one app running one AI on the computer you already have. Nothing ever leaves the machine. Like a calculator, but it writes code.

Best for

Trying local AI for the first time, working offline or on a plane, and keeping private code fully private.

The catch

One person at a time, and speed depends on your laptop. Good enough to learn on, not to serve a team.

In plain English

One strong computer in the corner does all the thinking. Everyone's tools phone it instead of the cloud, the way office printers share one machine.

Best for

A solo builder with a GPU box, or a small team that wants one shared, no-bill AI endpoint.

The catch

You now run a server. You tune batching and cache settings, and if the box is down, everyone is down.

In plain English

Do the everyday work at home for free, and only mail the few really hard problems to a paid expert. You get most of the savings and keep a safety valve.

Best for

Real production work where you want low cost and privacy by default, but still want a frontier model on call.

The catch

The router has to judge "hard" well, and anything sent to the cloud leaves your machine, so route deliberately.

In plain English

Instead of making the AI read the whole library to answer one question, it writes a little program to look things up and hands back just the answer. The window never clogs.

Best for

Giant or legacy codebases where stuffing everything into the prompt makes the model slow, expensive, and forgetful.

The catch

More moving parts to run, and it shines on big repos, not on a quick one-file edit.

In plain English

One foreman hands jobs to a crew. Each worker works in its own sealed room (so one cannot break another), and they all ask the same engine for thinking.

Best for

Big sweeps: reviewing a whole repo, fixing many files, or auditing in parallel instead of one step at a time.

The catch

Your job shifts from coding to herding. Coordination, not raw speed, becomes the bottleneck.

06

The Zero-Trust Sandbox

agents that touch your machine · the AI suggests, code decides

In plain English

The AI can ask to do things, but a bouncer checks every request, signs it, and runs it in a room you can undo. A bad command rewinds instead of wrecking your computer.

Best for

Any agent allowed to run commands, install packages, or edit files, especially unattended.

The catch

Extra setup, and the gate adds a small delay. Worth it the first time it stops a mistake.

In plain English

Several computers act like one big one. A manager sends each request to the best free worker and spins workers up or down as the team gets busy.

Best for

Serving a whole team or product with high availability, when one box is genuinely the bottleneck.

The catch

Real operations work: Kubernetes, failover, monitoring. Do not reach here until a single box is truly maxed out.

In plain English

Most decisions are easy, so a tiny brain handles them instantly right where they happen. Only the rare hard ones travel up to bigger, slower, more powerful brains.

Best for

Real-time and edge work where waiting on a GPU round-trip is too slow, and most calls are routine.

The catch

The most advanced and experimental of the eight. Great mental model today, deep engineering to build.

In plain English

The AI runs inside a web page, the way a video plays in a tab. Nothing to install, and nothing leaves the device. The simplest possible setup.

Best for

Phones, locked-down work laptops, quick demos, and fully offline use where simple is the whole point.

The catch

Only small models fit in a browser tab, so it handles light tasks, not heavy coding or long documents.

10

The Multi-Provider Router

never depend on one source · automatic failover

Your agent or app
↓

first choice, free and private
Cloud provider A
Cloud provider B

In plain English

One smart switchboard sits in front of several brains: your local one plus a couple of cloud options. If one is down, slow, or expensive, it quietly uses another. You never hang on a single provider.

Best for

Teams that cannot afford an outage, and anyone who wants to dodge any one vendor's price changes or sudden policy shifts.

The catch

More to configure, and you must make sure the truly private work still stays on the local path, not a cloud one.
